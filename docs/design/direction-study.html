Recommendation — C, with A as the safe alternative

You are being read by people who look at dark terminal sites all day. C is the only one of the three they will not have seen before, and its argument is structural rather than stylistic: inverting figure and ground makes the product the focal object instead of letting a dark screenshot dissolve into a dark page. It also keeps continuity with cmux's existing light brand, which reads as a considered redesign rather than a repaint.

B has the highest ceiling and the highest chance of missing. It is the most memorable, but it needs photography it does not have, and it forces a documented exception to the font rules. Choose it only if you want the piece to argue loudly.

A is the safe, competent answer. It will look right and it will look familiar, because it lives near Linear and Vercel. That is exactly its weakness for a portfolio piece whose job is to show taste rather than fluency.

Blocked on you

Two things before the build starts

  1. Pick a direction. Nothing else moves until this is settled, and only the chosen one gets implemented.
  2. Sign in to 21st.dev. Search and install are both gated behind auth, so run npx @21st-dev/cli login in your own terminal, or export TWENTYFIRST_TOKEN yourself. Do not paste the key into chat. The build is not blocked on this — it changes whether components come from the 21st registry or from the 243 skills already vendored here.

Shot list

Captures that would raise the ceiling

  1. A notification ring firing while an agent waits. The single most valuable shot — it is the one thing no competitor screenshot has.
  2. The sidebar with real workspaces, showing branches, PR status, directories and listening ports. Not the empty state.
  3. Split panes — terminal beside terminal, and terminal beside the in-app browser.
  4. The notification panel open with pending items, and the command palette on ⌘⇧P.
  5. The iOS companion on a phone, if the TestFlight build is on your device.

Every image slot gets built at final dimensions with a labelled placeholder, so real captures drop in later without touching layout. The terminal windows above are direction studies, not screenshots.
